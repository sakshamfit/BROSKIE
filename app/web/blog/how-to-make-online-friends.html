How to Make Online Friends (and Keep Them)

Online friends are real friends — if you start in the right room. Here's where to meet people online, how to go from a group chat to a one-to-one, and the five habits that turn a username into someone you'd actually call.

Most advice about making friends online is either “just put yourself out there” or a list of apps. Neither tells you the thing that actually matters: friendships form through repeated contact around a shared activity. Get those two ingredients right and the app barely matters. Get them wrong and no app will save you. So this guide is about the ingredients.

Start in a room about something, not about you

Profile-first apps ask you to be interesting on a blank page. Activity-first communities ask you to show up to a run, a study session, a trip plan or a game night — and the conversation writes itself. That's why the highest-yield place to make online friends is an interest-based community with a live group chat: you're never “messaging a stranger”, you're voting on Saturday's route.

Pick something you already do or genuinely want to start. Not the most popular room — the one you'd open on a tired Tuesday.

Show up in the same room, repeatedly

Familiarity is most of friendship. Open the same group chat a few times a week for a month. React. Vote in the poll. Answer the question nobody's answered. You don't need to be witty — you need to be recognisable. Around week three people start addressing you by name, and that's the moment everything gets easier.

Contribute before you ask

  • Share something useful: a route, a resource, a playlist, a photo from the trail, a voice note with the plan.
  • Say yes early: be one of the first names on the “who's in” poll. Organisers remember the people who make plans real.
  • Ask a small favour later: once you've given a few times, asking (“anyone got notes for chapter 4?”) becomes a bonding move rather than a cold request.

Move to one-to-one — with a reason

The jump from group to DM is where most online friendships stall. The fix is simple: message one person about one specific thing they said. Not “hi”, not “how are you” — “you mentioned the Sunday trail was muddy, which shoes did you use?” On Plus One, a first message to someone outside your contacts lands in their Message Requests, so they accept on their own time and nobody feels ambushed. Keep it about the plan; let it grow from there.

Make a plan, then follow up

Talk becomes friendship when it becomes time: a voice call, a run, a study block, a chai. After the plan, send one message that references it (“the second lap nearly killed me — same time next week?”). Two follow-ups later you have a friend, not a contact. If you're organising for a group, a pinned plan and a recurring poll do most of the staying-in-touch work for you.

Keeping online friends

  • Keep one shared room alive. A small GC of three to six people outlasts any 1:1 thread because someone's always around.
  • Switch modes sometimes. Voice notes and calls carry tone that text loses; a five-minute call is worth a week of messages.
  • Remember details. Their exam date, their trip, their dog's name. Star the message so you don't forget.
  • Let it fade and come back. Online friendships survive gaps; a “saw this and thought of you” after three quiet weeks is completely normal.

Staying safe

Keep control of contact. Prefer an app that doesn't need your phone number (Plus One signs you up with a username only), let unknown senders wait in a request inbox, don't post your address in a group you just joined, block and report without guilt, and meet in public the first time you take a friendship offline. Disappearing messages and secret chats are there if a conversation needs to stay light or private.

Online friends, answered

Are online friends real friends?

Yes. Friendship is repeated contact, shared experience and mutual help — none of which needs the same room. Plenty of close adult friendships start online, in a group around a shared interest, and move offline later or never.

How do I make online friends without being awkward?

Start in a group, not a DM. Vote, answer, react to something specific. When you message someone one-to-one, reference what they said instead of “hi”. Talking about a shared plan is never awkward.

Where can I make online friends for free?

Interest communities. Plus One's are free — running, travel, gaming, study groups, chai chats, nightlife, house parties — each with a real-time group chat, plus colleague discovery by college or workplace. See the app comparison for other options.

How long does it take?

A few weeks of regular presence in one room, then one or two one-to-one conversations with a plan attached. Consistency beats intensity.

How do I keep online friends?

Follow up after plans, keep one small shared group alive, switch to voice occasionally, and remember details. A pinned plan or a recurring poll does most of the work.

Is it safe to make friends online?

It is when you control contact: no phone number required, a request inbox for unknown senders, easy block and report, and public places when you meet offline. Those are Plus One's defaults.
