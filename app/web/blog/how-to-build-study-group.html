How to Build a Study Group That Actually Shows Up

Nobody failed because they didn’t know where to study. Here’s how to build a group that meets at the same time, in the same place, week after week.

The difference between a study group and a failing study group is rarely intelligence — it’s attendance. You can have the smartest set of people in the class, but if the group meets twice in a term, it’s not a study group, it’s a WhatsApp group with study in the name. The good news: showing up is a system, not a personality trait.

Find your people

You want the people with the same deadlines, not necessarily the same marks. Find them by circulating your usernames, or join a study-groups community on Plus One and start a room for your course. Keep it easy — you don’t need a formal application, just a group chat.

Keep it small

Three to six people is the sweet spot. Big enough that someone always turns up, small enough that everyone actually talks. Much past six and you’ve built a seminar — everyone listens, no one works.

Pick one slot and defend it

This is the big one. Don’t ask “when are we free?” every week — that’s what kills study groups. Run one poll to pick a recurring slot (say, Tuesday 6 PM), then defend that slot. Attendance becomes the default, not a negotiation.

Have an agenda

  • Set the topic before you meet. “We’re doing Chapter 4 today” — posted in the group chat the day before.
  • 90 minutes, then stop. Long enough to get into it, short enough that nobody dreads it.
  • Everyone teaches something. Ask each person to explain one part. You don’t really know something until you’ve had to explain it out loud.

Make it easy to show up

A room you can join from anywhere beats a room you have to travel to. Meet online when you can — plus, put the notes where the chat is. On Plus One you can pin the plan, share the notes in the same thread, and use a disappearing timer for the logistics so the room stays about studying. When showing up is frictionless, you rarely need to convince anyone to come.

Study groups, answered

How do I find a study group?

Look for people doing the same course with the same deadlines — then agree a fixed slot. On Plus One you can join a study-group community or create one and invite via a link.

How do I make a study group actually show up?

Keep it small, pick a fixed time and place, and make attendance the default rather than the exception. A poll to confirm the slot once is fine; polling every single week is how it dies.

How big should a study group be?

Three to six people. Big enough that someone always shows, small enough that everyone actually talks. A group of ten quickly becomes a lecture with snacks.

Should I use a group chat for a study group?

Yes — a group chat is the glue. Post the plan, set the topic, and use a poll to pick the slot. Share notes and resources in the same chat so everything’s in one place.

What makes a productive study group?

A fixed agenda, an agreed length (90 minutes works), and a clear “we’re doing this topic today”. Show up ready to teach what you’re covering — teaching is how it sticks.
