How to Make a Group Chat That Actually Sticks

Three taps, under a minute, free. Here’s how to create a GC on Plus One — and the small habits that stop it dying by Friday.

A group chat is the easiest room you’ll ever open and the hardest one to keep alive. Most people start one, add eleven people, and by Sunday it’s a ghost town of “who’s this?” and “why am I here?”. The good news: making a GC is genuinely easy, and the ones that survive share a handful of habits you can copy today.

Making a GC in three taps

On Plus One, group chats are the same screen you use for 1:1 chats — they’re just bigger. Open the app, tap new-chat, and choose New group instead of a single person. From there:

  1. Add people by username. No phone numbers, no contact-list upload, no “does anyone have their number?”. Type the username and they’re in — or queued behind a request if you don’t know them yet.
  2. Name it. This is the single most underrated step. A GC named “Weekend Goa” or “Sem 5 Physics” tells everyone why they’re there. “New Group” tells them nothing.
  3. Send one message that means something. Not “hi”. A plan, a question, a poll. The first message sets the tone for the whole room.

That’s genuinely it. There’s a step-by-step on the group chat page if you want the full walkthrough with screenshots in words.

The habits that keep a GC alive

Anyone can make a GC. Very few can make one that’s still being used next month. The difference is usually four things:

  • Start every real plan with a poll. “Which day?”, “veg or non-veg?”, “who’s in for 6 AM?”. A live poll with visible bars ends the argument before it starts. This is the number-one feature people don’t know they’re missing.
  • Pin the plan, not the meme. When a decision is made, pin it. The pinned message becomes the room’s front door — late joiners scroll up to it instead of asking what they missed.
  • Let logistics disappear. “Ok”, “seen”, “reached” — this is the noise that makes your phone buzz forty times an hour. Put it on a disappearing timer and it clutters nothing. Keep the banter permanent.
  • Keep it the right size. Ten to fifteen people is the sweet spot. Small enough to talk, big enough to plan. More than that and you’ve built a noticeboard, not a room.

Admin controls: the unglamorous stuff that matters

One admin who vanishes is how every group dies. On Plus One the last admin can never remove themself, so there’s always someone at the wheel. Admins can rename, promote others, and remove people — and you always know when someone joins or when the rules change. There’s no silent “added to random GC” surprise.

Making a group chat, answered

How do I make a group chat on Plus One?

Tap new-chat, choose New group, add members by username, name it and send. It takes under a minute and is completely free — no phone number needed.

Can I make a group chat without adding my contacts?

Yes. You add people by username, never by uploading your contact list. Your contacts stay on your phone.

What makes a good group chat?

The right size (small enough to talk, big enough to plan), a decision that sticks via a poll, a pinned message or two, and a way to quiet the noise like disappearing messages.

How many people can be in a group?

There’s no meaningful limit for a friend group — the design is for small, high-trust rooms of 10–50. Bigger open groups work better as a community.

Is making a group chat free?

Yes. Plus One has no ads, no in-app purchases and no premium tier. Group chats, polls and voice notes are all free.
